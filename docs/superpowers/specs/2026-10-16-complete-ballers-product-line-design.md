## Required content standard

Every new standalone product must have a concise original curriculum and reusable application tools. Target 18–30 substantive workbook pages for specialist guides and 14–22 for the flagship; these are production targets, never advertised page counts until finished files are measured. Quality and usability take precedence over page count.

Every product package contains:

1. A searchable, font-embedded printable PDF with clear instructions, usable writing space, real source notes and version/date.
2. An accessible offline HTML edition containing the same teaching, examples and blank tools. It must work without an account, API key, subscription, network or remote analytics. Browser storage must be opt-in with a clear erase/export function if any personal inputs are stored; static/printable worksheets are the default.
3. One genuinely useful editable tracker (CSV, or a validated spreadsheet when calculations add value), clearly labeled units and a fictional completed example.
4. START-HERE instructions, exact included-file list, personal-use license and support link to the site's existing contact route. Do not invent a support SLA or guarantee.

Each concept gets an application exercise. Before a blank worksheet, show one explicitly fictional worked example. Listings promise only files and features actually in the ZIP. No copied federation activity sheets, proprietary courses, employer collateral, client/student records, fabricated endorsements or invented credentials.

Soccer activities include purpose, setup, equipment alternatives, instructions, observation question, easier/harder variations and recording space. Technical work links to decisions and game-like play. Original diagrams use labeled positions and symbols with a legend, not decorative pitch art.

Life and flagship reflection remain non-diagnostic. Career downloads are self-guided and do not promise personalized job sourcing. Digital material includes provider-neutral principles and clearly dated tool examples; no claim of live AI, automatic integrations, compliance certification or implementation service in the purchase. Business remains the existing product, with no loan/grant approval implication.

## Soccer adaptation framework

Organize by age AND experience AND environment. These are editorial pathways rather than federation-prescribed divisions:

- Ages 6–8: adult-assisted, picture/game-led activities and brief reflection rather than written homework.
- Ages 9–12: simple player entries, varied roles and adjustable opposition/space.
- Ages 13–15: more independent planning with growth/maturation accommodations.
- Ages 16–18: player-owned goals and role discussions with adult support.
- Adults 18+, including recreational returners and competitive players: time/space constraints, team demands and personal starting point; no assumption that chronological age determines capacity.

Every soccer product includes recreational and competitive examples, plus low-equipment/limited-space alternatives. Competitive does not mean adult volume for children. Adaptation is explicit in cards and examples, not merely a sentence on the cover. Goalkeeper and outfield readers both receive relevant options where the subject applies; no claim this replaces position-specific coaching.

Physiological means participation, preparedness and general training literacy. Avoid bodyweight judgments, calorie prescriptions, diagnosis, rehabilitation protocols, return-to-play decisions and standardized exertion programs. Stop activity for concerning symptoms and seek suitable qualified support where relevant; do not turn all pages into generic warnings.

Psychological means practical reflection and routines; it is not therapy or a validated psychological assessment. Political means selection, roles, club systems, resource access and respectful advocacy, not partisan politics or pressure tactics. Minutes alone do not prove bias. Safeguarding concerns have independent reporting routes and do not require first confronting a coach. Adult-minor contact guidance follows current official policy, with jurisdiction identified.
