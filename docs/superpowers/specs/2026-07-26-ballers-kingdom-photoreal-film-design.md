# The Standard Starts Here — Production Design

**Approved:** 2026-07-26  
**Brand:** The Ballers Kingdom  
**Format:** 45-second landscape YouTube film, 1280×720, 24fps  
**Initial publishing path:** local review animatic → user approval → paid Veo scenes → unlisted review → explicit release decision

## Objective

Replace the rejected abstract tactics video with a grounded, photoreal soccer-development film. The audience should see real coaching, athletic effort, correction, confidence, and connection—not stock-style montage, diagrams, or a presenter talking at camera.

## Audience and action

Primary audience: young soccer athletes and their families in the Inland Empire.  
Secondary audience: coaches, schools, and community partners.

The finished film ends with the post-composited CTA: **Build Your Kingdom · ballkingdom.com**.

## Approved visual direction

Title: **The Standard Starts Here**.

Brian appears as the Ballers Kingdom coach. The user has authorized existing Ballers Kingdom Brian references to guide continuity only. The generated film must maintain one consistent, adult coach: Black man in his 30s; short natural hair; neatly trimmed beard; black Ballers Kingdom training jacket and black athletic pants; calm, precise coaching presence. Do not generate readable apparel/logo text; composited branding supplies all legible identity.

The athlete is an original 15–17-year-old male soccer player in a plain dark training kit. Supporting figures are an original teammate and a parent/guardian. The setting is a believable Southern California community soccer field at warm late-afternoon/golden-hour light.

## Locked five-beat story

| Time | Scene | Purpose | Narration / post copy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–8s | Arrival | Brian greets the athlete at the field gate; a ball is passed into the scene. | “The standard doesn’t start at game time.” |
| 8–17s | Correction | Brian demonstrates a first-touch detail; the athlete listens, repeats it, and earns a quiet nod. | “It starts with the rep no one is watching.” |
| 17–26s | Pressure | An alive small-sided drill shows a decision under pressure; teammate reacts and the athlete breaks free. | “Skill gives you a chance. Discipline makes you ready.” |
| 26–36s | Connection | Brian speaks briefly with the athlete and guardian at the sideline; the athlete re-enters with confidence. | “Because we’re building more than players.” |
| 36–45s | Invitation | Brian watches the athlete finish a drill; scene resolves to a clean post-composited end card. | “The Ballers Kingdom. Build your kingdom.” |

## Generation and post-production rules

- Use Veo/Gemini for five 8–10 second text-free scenes only after animatic approval.
- Generate each paid scene one time unless the user authorizes another take.
- Use the approved Brian reference only to stabilize likeness, wardrobe, and age; reject drift, broken hands, incorrect age, or false readable logos.
- Show real interaction in every scene: coach/athlete exchange, athlete response, teammate or guardian reaction, motivated camera movement, and active background life.
- Composite the title, brand, website, CTA, captions, and all other readable language in post.
- Use a natural, original narration treatment and licensed/original music/field ambience. No voice clone without a distinct approval.
- The final 7–9 seconds must carry an audible music/field bed through the CTA and an intentional fade—no silent tail.

## Quality and release gates

1. Build and validate the full `video-engine` package.
2. Render a no-cost local animatic using approved stills/placeholders, locked copy, timing, and post graphics.
3. Get user approval of the animatic before spending Veo credits.
4. Inspect representative frames from every paid scene before edit.
5. Pass technical/perceptual QA: H.264/AAC, 1280×720, 24fps, 45±1s, audio stream, readable CTA, continuity, local open/seek, and final-ten-second audio.
6. Upload only an unlisted review master after QA; change privacy only with an explicit later release decision.
