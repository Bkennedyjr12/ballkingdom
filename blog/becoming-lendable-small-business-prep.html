Becoming lendable: what small business owners should prep

October 15, 2026 · The Ballers Kingdom

"The plans of the diligent lead surely to abundance." (Proverbs 21:5) Preparation improves the conversation. It doesn't decide the outcome.

Know what lenders weigh

The SBA describes five common factors: creditworthiness, capacity (cash flow), capital invested, collateral, and conditions (SBA). Each lender weighs them differently.

1. Start with the owner's files

Most small-business lenders review owners too, especially for newer businesses. Pull your personal files first — see the files lenders actually see.

2. Look like a company

  • Entity registered and in good standing with your state; licenses current.
  • A free EIN from the IRS — never pay a third party for it.
  • A dedicated business bank account. Keep personal and business money separate.
  • A consistent name, address, and phone everywhere.
  • A free D-U-N-S Number from D&B and vendors that report your payments.

3. Build a document vault

Commonly requested: 2–3 years of business and personal tax returns, year-to-date profit & loss and balance sheet, recent bank statements, a debt schedule, entity documents, and licenses.

4. Know your capacity

Can the business carry the payment? Build a 12-month cash-flow view and understand your debt-service coverage before you ask.

5. Make a specific ask

Know the amount, the use of funds, and how it pays back. Free help is available from SBDCs and SCORE mentors via sba.gov/local-assistance.

Note: SBA updated its 7(a) and 504 underwriting rules effective October 1, 2026 — see our News page.

Educational only. This is general education, not financial, credit, or legal advice. No score change, loan approval, amount, or rate is promised. Under the law, only information that is inaccurate, incomplete, or unverifiable can be disputed; accurate, current information can't be removed by you or anyone else. Talk with a qualified professional about your situation.
