Build respectful outreach and evaluate internships through evidence, access, supervision and fit.

Your working format

Written lessons, fictional worked examples, practice templates, checks with explanations, and a final project.

Estimated working time: 185 minutes, including exercises. Work at your own pace.

A course-specific introduction with English captions is available below. The course video update includes 4 narrated worked examples, descriptive transcripts, written lessons and reviewed practice.

Who this is for

  • Students
  • Returning learners
  • Early-career adults

What you will build

Present an opportunity map, a rehearsed outreach draft, a two-opportunity comparison and a two-week learning agreement. Explain one access barrier and an equivalent route. No real reply or placement is required.

No paid software, public posting, real outreach or sale is required. Use a private real situation or a clearly labeled fictional case.

How you will know you learned

Complete an observable piece of work, check your reasoning, and revise it using an original instructional rubric. Return to a new case after 14 days.

This is educational practice. No accredited certification or admissions, employment, income or wealth result is promised.

Welcome to Relationships into Opportunity. I’m Brian Kennedy Jr. Relationships can make education and entrepreneurship an adventure, but they also involve real limits, unequal access and other people’s choices. We will work with that honestly. You will start with a question you want to understand, find appropriate people or resources, and practice a small, respectful request. Then you will compare internships and other experiences by the work, feedback, costs and support they actually offer. The goal is to build useful relationships and make informed choices. You do not need a famous contact, a reply or an internship offer to demonstrate learning here. You need preparation, a clear request, an accurate record and a next step. You can practice through role-play if contacting someone is not appropriate. Open the first lesson and write one question about a field, role or problem you want to explore.
