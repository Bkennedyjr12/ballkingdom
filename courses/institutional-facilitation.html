Facilitate Education and Enterprise Learning

Prepare, deliver and evaluate a bounded pilot using original performance assessments and learner choice.

By Brian Kennedy Jr., M.Ed.

Ask about institutional delivery

Scope and institutional use quoted separately.

Your working format

Written lessons, fictional worked examples, practice templates, checks with explanations, and a final project.

Estimated working time: 185 minutes, including exercises. Work at your own pace.

A course-specific introduction with English captions is available below. The course video update includes 4 narrated worked examples, descriptive transcripts, written lessons and reviewed practice. Group delivery and institutional use are scoped separately.

Who this is for

  • College staff
  • Educators
  • Community program facilitators

What you will build

Deliver a pilot charter, accessible session plan, minimal evidence/scoring plan and next-cycle decision memo. Rehearse one demonstration and score two fictional artifacts. No institutional certification, accreditation or effectiveness claim is conferred.

No paid software, public posting, real outreach or sale is required. Use a private real situation or a clearly labeled fictional case.

How you will know you learned

Complete an observable piece of work, check your reasoning, and revise it using an original instructional rubric. Return to a new case after 14 days.

This is educational practice. No accredited certification or admissions, employment, income or wealth result is promised.

The learning sequence

  1. Define a pilot and a performance outcome

    Build: Pilot charter and assessment map

  2. Teach with demonstration, practice, and a debrief

    Build: Accessible session plan

  3. Collect useful evidence with consent and restraint

    Build: Privacy-aware evidence and scoring plan

  4. Decide whether to revise, repeat, or expand

    Build: Pilot review and next-cycle plan

A free practice sample

Read the exact introduction transcript

Welcome to Facilitate Education and Enterprise Learning. I’m Brian Kennedy Jr. This course is for educators and program teams who want learners to leave with work they can use. You will define a focused pilot, plan a demonstration and practice sequence, and assess observable performance through an original rubric. We will make room for different access needs and for learners who choose private examples instead of public sharing, outreach or selling. You will also build a minimal evidence plan that keeps consent, missing work and the limits of a small pilot visible. The result is a delivery package and a next-cycle decision, not a claim of accreditation or proven long-term impact. Start by choosing one primary learner group and one artifact they should be able to produce. That choice will guide the session, the assessment and the evidence you collect.
