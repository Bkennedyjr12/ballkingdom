First Customer Experiment

Test a small offer, deliver responsibly, and record the economics without inventing demand.

By Brian Kennedy Jr., M.Ed.

Your working format

Written lessons, fictional worked examples, practice templates, checks with explanations, and a final project.

Estimated working time: 185 minutes, including exercises. Work at your own pace.

Course-specific videos are not included in this edition. The shared Brian avatar welcome is available below.

Who this is for

  • Students
  • Adults exploring self-employment
  • Early-stage entrepreneurs

What you will build

Submit a problem record, bounded offer, test rule, delivery/economics log and evidence-based review. Real commerce is optional; label simulations and zero-sale outcomes. Explain the difference between a compliment, commitment, cash received and sustainable income.

No paid software, public posting, real outreach or sale is required. Use a private real situation or a clearly labeled fictional case.

How you will know you learned

Complete an observable piece of work, check your reasoning, and revise it using an original instructional rubric. Return to a new case after 14 days.

This is educational practice. No accredited certification or admissions, employment, income or wealth result is promised.

The learning sequence

  1. Find a problem you can investigate

    Build: Problem discovery record

  2. Define a bounded offer and a test

    Build: Offer and experiment card

  3. Deliver, get acceptance, and record the numbers

    Build: Delivery and economics log

  4. Decide what to repeat, change, or stop

    Build: Experiment review and next test

A free practice sample

Start with a decision you can name.

Try these first three prompts from “Find a problem you can investigate.” Use a notebook or your preferred accessible format. Your answers stay with you; this page collects no worksheet responses.

  1. Audience / situation / hypothesis
  2. Three recent-behavior questions
  3. Consent and note limits
Check your reasoning

Does “that sounds cool” prove someone will buy your offer?

No. It is a reaction, not a commitment or purchase.
